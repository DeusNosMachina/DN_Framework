FIELD NOTES

A carried reading becomes available as context for a later reading. The next observer encounters the present signals alongside an inheritance of earlier selections. This can make a relationship easier to recognize. It can also make another reading harder to maintain.

Here, inheritance is specific: two glyph readings can exchange influence only through binary pairs that both readings include. Select a parent in the story to remove that contribution and replay everything that follows.

This is an authored model of interpretive influence. Its weights are choices. Repeated or inherited readings are not counted as new, independent evidence.

Two observation fields can include the same signal while preserving different relationships within it. One reader emphasizes equal neighboring states; the other emphasizes changes. These are interpretive preferences. They are not two competing measurements of the bits.

Solid and dashed contours keep the readers attributable. Carrying from both fields requires shared attention to a glyph; it does not require agreement about its reading. A shared context can affect both contours without producing a group verdict.

The faint wash and rings preview eligible glyphs: the union for Either, the overlap for Both. On Carry, the highlight strengthens around the actual selection. With sound enabled, participating readers contribute their material voices, with an added fifth for the second reader: staggered by 90 milliseconds for Either, simultaneous and centered on the shared selection for Both. A reader who carries nothing contributes no Carry tone. Ripples may still sound.

Each reader’s latest carried version of a glyph remains available to the shared context. Its influence decreases with the rounds since it was carried. Earlier versions and every captured binary state remain in the archive.

Visit any layer and return a signal. Its earlier record stays intact; a copy enters the present. On the next Carry, that record meets a different context. Fading here means reduced influence and visibility—not the destruction of a stored state.

History is kept on this device when browser storage permits. Download it to keep a portable copy. Reset field starts a new personal history after confirmation; refreshing or reopening the page restores the saved session. A public site does not make visitors’ separate sessions a shared multiplayer session.

Removing a contribution suppresses one specific carry, then recomputes the later layers. The observation at that layer still happens. If an older carried version existed, it remains available and continues to lose influence.

The replay uses the same recorded fields, preferences, edits, retrieval actions, binary settings, and random seeds. A dotted contour shows the alternative beside the actual reading. Differences identify consequences under this model. They cannot tell us how people would have chosen differently after a different past.

A small or zero effect does not prove a contribution is irrelevant in every possible context. Other contributions can duplicate or counteract it. The archive retains those dependencies too.

The stored states are 0 and 1. The alphabet chooses how they appear. The other visual pair is an unarticulated scribble and an articulated reading. A glyph is a drawing of relationships; it has no preassigned word or universal meaning.

Each observed glyph reads adjacent binary pairs inside its observer’s field and within a fixed local neighborhood. Equal states support repetition; unequal states support interruption. Relationships contribute in their direction from the glyph. Nearer pairs have greater weight.

For each pair, the current local reading has weight 1. Each observer has twelve glyph slots sharing an inherited-voice budget of 0.55. A relevant carried glyph supplies its slot’s share; an unused slot stays unused. Age multiplies influence by 0.72 per elapsed round. The weighted reading controls eight contour directions. Tension between the local and inherited readings leaves more of the original spiral visible.

Attention alone does not progressively polish a glyph. Unchanged inputs and unchanged inherited influence produce the same contour. A retained reading can change outside attention when shared context changes; new raw evidence enters that reading only when it is observed again.

The radii, neighborhood, influence budget, and decay are declared drawing conventions, not measurements of intelligence, confidence, or social truth.

A thread names an earlier carried record that participates in a later reading through shared binary relationships. Its endpoints are records, not a match between similar-looking glyphs.

The default view follows one reader of one glyph. It shows turning points whose contour change exceeds 0.012 in this model’s feature distance, plus first encounters and returns. Up to three incoming parents are highlighted, ranked by the immediate change when each is individually removed and the reading is recomputed. All parents remain inspectable.

The longer replay tests an earlier carry across the subsequent history. It can reveal a consequence that passes through several other glyphs before reaching the selected one. Quiet layers remain accessible on the layer slider.

The visible story is a selection from the provenance. The selection rule is part of this expression and can be questioned with it.

In Ripple mode, touch or drag across the field. Each disturbance spreads through neighboring points. Waves add where they meet, sometimes reinforcing and sometimes canceling. Strength changes the initial disturbance; speed changes its travel; damping determines how soon it settles. Still the water clears the disturbance immediately.

The surface is a small numerical wave grid. Each next height combines the two previous heights and the difference from its four neighbors. It runs in fixed time steps, with damping and absorbing edges. This is a water-like wave model, not a full simulation of flowing fluid.

Visit an earlier layer and ripple it. Adjacent layers receive a delayed, weaker display response. This coupling is an authored way of feeling depth. It does not travel along the causal threads or revise the signal archive. Carry makes a new signal record; a ripple passes over one. With Save new touches enabled, that gesture is kept in a separate performance ledger.

Enable Sound, then touch the field. As a wave reaches signals, their positions spread notes across stereo space. Wave height shapes loudness and slightly bends pitch. Binary state adds a pitch interval; each site also has an assigned note. Carry adds lower tones for the readers who actually carried glyphs. Either separates their entrances; Both joins them. This cue marks shared attention, not agreement.

Ripple tones, Carry tones, and ambience have separate levels. Choose a base pitch, brightness, note length, rhythm density, echo space, stereo width, and a water, warm, or glass timbre. Master volume controls the whole mix. Ambience starts silent. Sound stays off until you enable it.

These are choices for sonification, not the sound of a meaning being discovered. Rhythm changes how often a site may sound; it is not a fixed musical tempo. M toggles sound. Reduced-motion preferences suppress the visual wave while allowing sound.

Enable binary evolution, choose the rules, and Carry. Positive coupling favors matching neighbors; negative coupling favors opposition. Resistance favors the current state. Bias favors 1 or 0. Agitation allows less likely changes. Choose four edge neighbors or include the diagonals; the field has boundaries, and glyph sites do not act as binary neighbors.

All bits update together from the same prior field. Their 0/1 states become −1/+1 polarities. Neighbor pressure is a weighted average, multiplied by coupling and shifted by bias. Flip probability decreases as the current polarity agrees with that pressure or resistance rises. A recorded random seed makes each round replayable. An explicit bit edit is held for that round.

Select a changed bit to inspect its pressure, flip chance, and recorded random draw. These changes can alter the binary pairs that observed glyphs read. Glyph interpretation still uses horizontal and vertical pairs; choosing diagonal binary interactions does not change that reading grammar.

This is a classical coupled binary model. It does not simulate quantum entanglement. The interaction settings and randomness are part of the recorded action; touching water does not flip a bit.

State shows the marks. Relations connects matching and opposing bits, with brightness showing pressure under the chosen interaction settings. Change reveals what differed in this round. Memory reveals recent active glyph readings; dimness does not mean a record has disappeared.

Frequency inspects how each bit varied across the layers up to the one you are visiting. Its dial selects a discrete Fourier frequency: cycles per recorded round. A steady bit stays dim. A repeating variation can become bright at a corresponding frequency. At least four layers are needed; longer histories offer finer resolution. Glyphs remain visible as context.

Columns examine eight neighboring frequency bands, from 40 Hz at the left to 20 kHz (or the decoding limit) at the right. Rows use the chosen threshold plus (row − 3.5) × row spread. The 12 glyphs keep their existing relationship and inherited-context rules.

Analysis uses a 2,048-sample Hann window with a 1,024-sample step. Stereo power is averaged, so opposite channel phases do not cancel. Band energy is RMS; activity divides it by that band’s 95th-percentile energy across the whole recording, clipped at 1. Relative strength is the band’s share of total measured power. Texture is spectral flatness: geometric mean divided by arithmetic mean of bin power within a band. Silence is 0.

Event duration is the full length of a contiguous band event above a fixed 20% analysis floor. Short events below your duration threshold map to 1 (dot); sustained events and gaps map to 0 (dash). This convention does not preserve the distinction between a gap and a sustained event. Activity + texture assigns activity to the top four rows and flatness to the bottom four, using Freeform marks.

Measurements below 0.00001 RMS always map to 0. Other values equal to their threshold map to 1; short-event duration uses a strict less-than comparison. Source input comes first, optional neighbor simulation follows, and explicit user edits are pinned last. An alphabet-only change leaves this reduction intact.

Spectral-change sampling distributes observations by positive changes in band RMS, with a small equal-time floor. It is not a beat estimate. Carries append in compute order, even after seeking backward. Source-time playback sorts one reading by timestamp; observations at the same time sound together. Comparison starts from the first reading’s pre-source context. All old layers remain inspectable.
